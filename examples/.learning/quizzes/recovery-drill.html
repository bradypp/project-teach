Recovery drill: two workers, one result

Created 16th Sept 2026 · 22:32

A model call returns after its worker has lost ownership. Decide what the platform should accept, what graph replay can change, and how the outcome should appear in evaluation.

The incident timeline

09:17:00  job accepted; deadline 09:17:30
09:17:02  A claims generation 7; lease ends 09:17:10
09:17:12  B claims generation 8; lease ends 09:17:25
09:17:13  A receives an answer citing runbook v7
09:17:14  cancellation commits; job status becomes cancelled
09:17:15  B receives an answer citing runbook v8

The proposed result-write contract requires running status, a current generation, a live lease and an unexpired job deadline. Every step is independently answerable.

1. Which completion may be accepted under this contract?
A hint

Check all of the required conditions, not only the generation.

Why this works

A has generation 7 after the store advanced to 8. B has the current generation but the status is cancelled. Both guarded writes must affect zero rows. Review ownership at the write.

2. Why might replay return runbook v8 instead of v7?
Compare your reasoning

A repeated lookup of a latest-version alias can see changed content. Pin the original version for a snapshot investigation, or explicitly record a refresh and its new time scope. Checkpointing does not make a live data source immutable. Review evidence identity.

Compare the calculation

Coverage is 6/10 = 60%. Support among answers is 4/6, about 66.7%. We cannot calculate appropriate abstention without knowing whether the four unanswered cases had sufficient evidence. Revisit the denominators.

4. Which observation tests stale-worker protection?
A hint

Why this works

The rejected write directly checks stale ownership. Identical answer text does not prove that the store rejected a late worker or enforced cancellation. Try the race.
