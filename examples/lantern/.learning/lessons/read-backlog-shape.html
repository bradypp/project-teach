Read the shape of a backlog

Created 13th Sept 2026 · 12:48

Queue depth is a snapshot. Pair it with arrival rate, completion rate and oldest-event age to tell whether Lantern is absorbing a burst, recovering, or quietly falling behind.

Read the curve Interrogate a snapshot Alert on meaning

A healthy burst has a descending side

This sample begins empty. For four minutes arrivals exceed completions, then the burst ends and the worker drains the backlog. The peak is not automatically a failure; the recovery slope and age limit decide whether users receive work within the promised time.

A flat queue is not necessarily a healthy queue. Equal arrival and completion rates can hold old work at a permanently late level. Age reveals the promise that depth alone hides.

Read rates before inventing a story

At 12:05 the count is lower than a minute ago, yet the oldest event is older. That combination deserves investigation rather than a generic “backlog improving” conclusion.

Alert on the contract, then add diagnostic signals

If Lantern promises to begin normal webhook processing within five minutes, oldest-event age is the closest queue signal to that promise. Depth, rate difference, attempts and rejects explain why the promise is threatened; they should not replace the service-level symptom.

  • Warn when oldest-event age consumes a useful fraction of the five-minute budget.
  • Page when age breaches the budget and continues rising across several samples.
  • Group depth and rate alerts by provider only when that split leads to an operational action.
  • Track discarded, rejected and dead-lettered events separately so a falling queue cannot masquerade as recovery.
