Bound the inbox

Created 13th Sept 2026 · 00:47

A queue moves waiting somewhere; it does not create processing capacity. Lantern needs a visible backlog limit and an honest policy for the moment arrivals outrun the worker.

See the pressure Separate two bounds Choose a policy Measure the right signals

The backlog is subtraction over time

If Lantern receives 90 events per second while its worker completes 60, the pending inbox grows by roughly 30 each second. A queue smooths a short spike; during sustained overload it merely delays the point of failure and makes the work older.

Backpressure is a contract, not a container. It says how intake responds when downstream capacity is exhausted: wait, reject, shed, redirect, or deliberately degrade.

Lantern has two different queues

The SQLite inbox owns accepted events. An asyncio.Queue can cheaply wake local workers, but it must not become the only copy. Python documents that a positive maxsize makes await put() wait when the queue is full. 

wakeups = asyncio.Queue(maxsize=1)

async def nudge_worker():
    try:
        wakeups.put_nowait(None)
    except asyncio.QueueFull:
        pass  # one wake-up is already pending; events remain in SQLite

async def worker():
    while True:
        try:
            await asyncio.wait_for(wakeups.get(), timeout=2)
        except TimeoutError:
            pass  # periodic polling also finds work after a restart

        while event := inbox.claim_next():
            await process(event)

Bounding the wake-up queue protects process memory. It does not bound the durable backlog. Lantern therefore also needs a product-level limit—perhaps maximum pending rows, maximum oldest-event age, or a per-provider quota—checked before it accepts new ownership.

Choose overload behavior deliberately

PolicyGood fitCost
Reject before storing with 503 and Retry-After The provider retries and delivery latency can stretch. Retry traffic may amplify an incident; coordinate limits and backoff.
Spill into another durable tier Burst retention matters more than immediate processing. More infrastructure and a recovery path to operate.
Drop expired or low-value work The event has an explicit freshness or priority contract. Data loss must be intentional, observable and acceptable.
Accept without a bound Almost never a real policy. Disk exhaustion and stale work choose the failure mode for you.

The ordering matters: if Lantern chooses rejection, it must reject before the acknowledgement boundary. Once the row is committed and success is returned, Lantern owns the work even if the worker is slow.

Depth tells you how much; age tells you how late

  • Pending depth shows the amount of unfinished work.
  • Oldest-event age exposes user-visible delay even when volume is modest.
  • Arrival and completion rates show whether the system is recovering or falling further behind.
  • Reject and retry rates reveal pressure moved back to providers.
  • Attempt count and dead-letter volume separate poison events from broad saturation.
