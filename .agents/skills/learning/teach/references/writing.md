# Writing for a learner

Use these rules while drafting explanations, lessons, practice and feedback.

## Be clear and specific

- Explain the actual mechanism with a concrete project example.
- Use familiar words; introduce necessary technical terms precisely.
- Keep supported facts, meaningful uncertainty and citations intact.
- Start with the useful point when an introduction adds nothing.

## Sound like a thoughtful teacher

- Address the learner directly, with warmth and honest curiosity.
- Challenge reasoning kindly; make feedback specific to the answer.
- Use humour, analogies and asides when they help understanding.
- Express judgment with reasons. Avoid invented experiences or unsupported claims.

## Keep the prose natural

- Give each sentence useful work; cut repetition and generic encouragement.
- Vary sentence length and structure to suit the explanation.
- Use headings and bullets for navigation; reserve emphasis for what matters.
- Replace inflated claims and promotional language with concrete observations.
- Keep comparisons that clarify real alternatives; remove rhetorical contrasts against imaginary objections.
- Preserve nuance and engaging examples while simplifying tangled wording.
